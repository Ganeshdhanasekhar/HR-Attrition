# HR-Attrition
Analyzing employee attrition across 1,470 employees using the HR dataset. Includes SQL schema, DAX measures, and interactive visuals covering attrition by role, department, overtime, age, and satisfaction — uncovering key drivers 

Attrition rate: 0.16 (about 16%). Roughly 16.12% of employees left, and 83.88% stayed.
Average tenure: 7.01 years
Average age: 36.92 years

Visuals and insights:

Attrition by department: Sales has the highest attrition rate (0.21), followed by Human Resources (0.19) and Research & Development (0.14).
Attrition by commute distance: Attrition is highest among employees living 1–5 km away and falls as the distance bucket increases. This is partly because most employees live in that bucket, so it is worth checking the rate, not just the count.
Education profile: Most employees hold a bachelor’s degree (572), then a master’s (398), college (282), below college (170), and doctorate (48).
Average monthly income by department: Salaries are fairly similar across departments (about 6–7K). Sales is slightly highest, so pay alone does not explain Sales’ higher attrition.

Takeaway: Attrition is concentrated in Sales and HR, so retention efforts should focus there, looking at workload, career growth, and engagement rather than only compensation.

Tools: Power BI, DAX measures (attrition rate, average tenure, average age), data cleaning and bucketing (distance buckets, education labels).
